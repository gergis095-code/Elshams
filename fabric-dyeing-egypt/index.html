What we dye

A dyehouse built around the buyer's shade, not our own range.

Most dyehouses sell you what is already on the floor. El Shams starts from your pantone, your lab dip or the physical swatch you post to us, and matches it before a single metre of bulk goes into the machine. Forty-five years of plain-dyed production under Mr Shawky Mokhtar has made that the habit rather than the exception.

Greige cloth arrives at the Sharkia mill, is inspected for weight, weave and shade consistency, and is scoured and bleached so it will take colour evenly across the full width. Only then does it go to dye. That pre-treatment step is where most shade complaints are actually born, and it is the step a cheap dyehouse shortens.

Fibres and blends we run

  • 100% cotton — woven and knitted, including denim cotton and cotton twill
  • Viscose and cotton/viscose blends
  • Polyester and poly-cotton

Machinery

Batch or continuous — chosen from your order.

A modern and varied setup means the machine is matched to the job in front of it rather than the job being forced onto whatever is free.

Jet dyeing

Batch dyeing for knitted and lighter woven cloth, with good liquor circulation and even shade development.

  • Suits medium and deep shades
  • Gentle on stretch blends

Banana jets

Rope-form batch dyeing for fabrics that need low tension through the machine.

  • Low-tension handling
  • Reduces crease marking

Jigger dyeing

Open-width batch dyeing for woven cloth where the fabric must stay flat throughout.

  • Open-width, no rope creases
  • Good for heavier wovens

Continuous dyeing

For long runs of a single shade, where consistency over thousands of metres matters more than lot flexibility.

  • Long-run economy
  • Shade held across the run

How a dyeing order moves

From your swatch to graded rolls.

01

Shade submission

You send a pantone, a lab dip or a physical swatch with the fabric construction and quantity.

02

Lab dip & approval

We match the shade and send it back. Nothing goes to bulk until you approve it.

03

Intake & pre-treatment

Greige is inspected, then scoured and bleached so colour takes evenly across the width.

04

Bulk dyeing

Batch or continuous, on the machine matched to your fibre and lot size.

05

Finishing

Mechanical and chemical finishing on compactors and stenters for hand-feel and stability.

06

QC & dispatch

Every roll inspected, graded and packed to destination spec, logged against your order.

Why buyers stay

Three things that decide a dyeing supplier.

Shade consistency

Dyeing, printing and finishing happen in one building, so a roll is not handed between mills with a shade drifting at every gate. Each stage is logged against the order, which means a fault can be traced back to where it actually happened.

Certified process

El Shams is certified to ISO 9001:2015 for quality management, ISO 14001:2015 for environmental management and ISO 45001:2018 for occupational health and safety — the three a European or Gulf buyer will ask for during audit.

New varieties on request

The standard plain-dyed range covers the everyday order. When a buyer needs something the range does not yet cover, it gets developed rather than refused.

Common questions

Before you send a spec

What fibres can you dye?

Cotton, viscose, polyester and blends including cotton/viscose, poly-cotton, cotton/lycra and denim cotton. If you are running something outside that range, send the construction and we will tell you honestly whether the line suits it.

Can you match a shade we already use?

Yes. Send a pantone reference, a lab dip or a physical swatch and we match it before bulk dyeing begins. Matching from a physical swatch is the most reliable route, because it carries the fibre and the finish as well as the colour.

Do you dye in batch or continuous?

Both. Batch dyeing on jets, banana jets and jiggers suits smaller lots and deep shades; continuous dyeing suits long runs of the same shade. We pick the route from your quantity and the fabric, not the other way round.

What is the minimum order?

It depends on the fibre and the dye class, because a jet has a working load below which shade control suffers. Tell us the quantity you have in mind and we will confirm whether it runs economically.

How long does an order take?

Lead time depends on shade, quantity and whether printing and finishing follow. We quote a firm lead time with the price, usually within two working days of receiving your spec.

Do you export?

Yes. We supply textile mills and ready-made garment manufacturers in Egypt and abroad, and we pack and grade every roll to destination spec.
