Why it matters

The stage buyers notice only when it goes wrong.

Nobody writes a complaint about good finishing. They write about a shirt that shrank two sizes, a twill that curled on the cutting table, or a jersey that arrived with the handle of cardboard. Finishing is the last thing done to cloth and the first thing the customer feels.

At El Shams finishing is not a service bolted onto the end of a dyeing order. It is the third of three disciplines that run in one building, which is why cloth can go from greige to cut-ready without leaving the mill — and why the finishing recipe can be set knowing exactly what the dyeing stage already did to the fabric.

What the finishing line covers

  • Stenter setting — width setting, heat setting and drying to a controlled dimension
  • Compacting — length stabilisation and residual shrinkage control, especially on knits
  • Chemical finishing — softeners and performance finishes chosen from the end use
  • Inspection and grading — every roll checked, graded and packed to destination spec

Machinery

Stenters and compactors, matched to the cloth.

Stenter

Sets the fabric to a controlled width while drying and heat-setting it. This is where the handle of a woven cloth is largely decided, and where a chemical finish is fixed into the fibre.

  • Width and dimensional setting
  • Heat setting for synthetics and blends
  • Application of chemical finishes

Compactor

Stabilises the length of knitted cloth so the residual shrinkage that reaches the garment maker is the figure on the spec, not a surprise after the first wash.

  • Residual shrinkage control
  • Improved surface and handle on knits
  • Consistent yardage per roll

Chemical finishing

Softeners and functional finishes selected from what the cloth is going to become — a shirting, a workwear twill and a jersey each get a different recipe.

  • Handle tuned to the end use
  • Performance finishes on request
  • Recipe recorded against the order

Common questions

Before you send a spec

What does finishing actually change?

Three things a garment maker cares about: hand-feel, dimensional stability and appearance. Finishing is what stops a shirt shrinking after the first wash, what makes cloth sit flat on a cutting table, and what gives a fabric the handle the buyer expected when they approved the swatch.

Can you finish cloth we dyed elsewhere?

Yes, we take in cloth for finishing alone. Send the construction, what was done to it already and the finish you need. Be aware that when the dyeing happened elsewhere we can only be accountable for the finishing stage.

How do you control shrinkage?

Compacting and controlled stenter setting bring the fabric to a stable width and length before it leaves the mill, and residual shrinkage is checked against the figure in your spec rather than against a house standard.

What finishes can you apply?

Mechanical finishing on stenters and compactors, plus chemical finishing for handle and performance. The recipe is set from the end use — a shirting, a denim twill and a knitted jersey do not want the same treatment.

Is every roll checked?

Yes. Every roll is inspected, graded and packed to destination spec, and the result is logged against the order.

Do you finish knits as well as wovens?

Both. Compactors handle knitted cloth where length stability is the issue; stenters handle woven cloth where width setting and handle matter most.

What finishing machinery do you run?

5 stenters, 2 closed compactors, a sanforizing machine, a singeing machine, 2 raising machines, a shaving machine, 4 squeezers, a dryer and a carbon finishing machine.

What is the minimum order?

250 kg per colour when we dye the cloth as well. For finishing-only work, send the quantity and we will confirm whether it runs economically.
